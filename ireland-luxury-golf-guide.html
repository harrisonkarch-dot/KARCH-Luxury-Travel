Golf Travel

How I would shape a luxury golf journey through Ireland, without letting it feel rushed.

Ireland is one of the few golf destinations where the landscape does half the work. The courses have genuine character, the coastline is dramatic in a way photographs undersell, and the hospitality is the actual reason people go back. The mistake is treating it as a course-collecting exercise. Seven rounds in seven days is not a golf trip, it is a commute with clubs.

What follows assumes eight nights and five rounds, which is the ratio I would defend. It leaves room for the parts people end up talking about afterwards.

Pick one region, not the whole country

The single decision that determines whether the trip works.

Ireland looks small on a map and drives large. Southwest to Northwest is five hours on roads that are genuinely beautiful and genuinely slow. Trying to cover both means three of your eight days happen through a windscreen.

The Southwest is the strongest single region — Adare, Ballybunion, Lahinch, Old Head, Waterville and Tralee sit within comfortable reach of each other, and the hotels are the best in the country. This is where I would send almost everyone on a first trip.

Dublin and the east works if you want city time on either end. Portmarnock and The Island are excellent, the logistics are simpler, and you can fly in and out of one airport. Less dramatic, considerably easier.

The Northwest and Northern Ireland — Royal County Down, Royal Portrush, Ballyliffin — is for the second trip, or for someone who genuinely wants the best links golf in the world and will accept the drive to get it.

The courses worth building around

Five, and what each one is actually for.

Adare Manor. The stay-and-play anchor, and the most refined golf hotel in Ireland by a distance. Worth knowing: it hosts the Ryder Cup in 2027, which means rates and availability tighten considerably as that approaches. If Adare is the centrepiece, book further ahead than feels reasonable.

Ballybunion Old Course. The one most people rank first once they have played it. Dunes, exposure, and holes that run along the Atlantic rather than near it.

Lahinch. Old-world character, a town that feels like a golf town, and more personality than polish. People either love it immediately or need a second nine to come round.

Old Head of Kinsale. On a promontory 300 feet above the Atlantic on three sides. Not the best course on this list and comfortably the most extraordinary setting.

Waterville or Tralee. Either, not both, unless you are staying in the far southwest. Both are strong; Tralee is easier, Waterville is more remote and more rewarding.

Where to stay

Two bases beat five hotels.

Adare Manor for the first half is close to unimprovable — the rooms, the service, and the fact that you can walk to the first tee. From there, a second base in Kerry or Clare puts the remaining courses within an hour.

Ashford Castle and Dromoland Castle both work if the trip is as much about the country as the golf, and if there are non-golfers in the group. Both have enough to do that someone who never picks up a club has a genuinely good week.

The things people get wrong

Four, in order of how much they cost you.

Too many rounds. Five in eight days leaves space for weather, recovery, and the day someone decides they would rather see the Cliffs of Moher. Seven leaves none.

Skipping caddies. On links courses you cannot see where you are going, and local knowledge is worth several shots a round. It is also, reliably, where the best stories of the trip come from.

Underestimating weather. It will rain. Plan for a round in it rather than around it, and bring the right kit — a wet round on a links course in proper gear is a genuinely good day.

Booking too late. The best tee times at the marquee courses go a year out for peak season. May, June and September are the months to aim for; July and August are busiest and no better.

When to go

May, June, or September.

September is my preference — the courses are in their best condition after a full season, the crowds thin after the first week, and the light in the evenings is long enough for a late nine. May and June are drier on average. April and October are gambles that sometimes pay off beautifully and sometimes do not.

KARCH Point of View

Ireland is strongest when it feels curated rather than crowded. The right trip balances legendary golf with beautiful hotels, calm transfers, and enough breathing room that the whole journey still feels indulgent.
