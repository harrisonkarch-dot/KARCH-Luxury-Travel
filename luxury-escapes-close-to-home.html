Journal

You don't need a passport for this: five absurdly luxurious hotels hiding in plain sight.

There's a very specific kind of tired that a long-haul flight cannot fix. You know the one — where what you actually need isn't a new time zone, it's silence, a good bathtub, and someone else deciding what's for dinner.

Turns out you don't need to cross an ocean for that. You need one of these five places, all of which are somehow both absolutely nowhere and exactly where you want to be.

Twin Farms, Vermont

For the couple who wants zero decisions made.

Ten cottages, 300 private acres, and a philosophy that can basically be summarized as "we'll handle it." There's no menu at dinner — the chef and the wine cellar just decide you're having something incredible tonight, and you go along with it. This is the kind of place where the biggest choice you'll make all weekend is whether to hike first or nap first. Correct answer: nap first.

Winvian Farm, Connecticut

For people who think "themed cottage" sounds cheesy until they see the helicopter.

Two hours from New York City is a working farm turned five-star resort where all 18 cottages were designed by different architects with, apparently, zero creative limits. One is a fully restored 1968 helicopter you sleep in. Another is a treehouse 35 feet in the air. It sounds like a gimmick until you realize the food, the spa, and the service are just as serious as the whimsy. Bring the kids or don't — either way, somebody's going to want a photo in the helicopter.

The Point, Adirondacks

For anyone who wants an entire lake to feel like it's theirs.

This one used to be a Rockefeller family retreat, and they clearly knew what they were doing, because it's basically perfect: open fires, canoes, a private lake, and so few rooms that it never feels like a "resort" at all — more like you very fortunately know someone with a spectacular lake house. If your idea of luxury is quiet, this is the one.

Mohonk Mountain House, New York

For the family trip that still feels fancy.

A genuine Victorian castle sitting on its own lake, surrounded by 40,000 acres that nobody's allowed to touch. This is the most kid-friendly option on the list by a wide margin — there's enough space and enough to do that everyone under 12 will be thrilled, and everyone over 40 will still feel like they're somewhere special. Rare combo.

Blackberry Farm, Tennessee

For when you want the "worth the flight" version of all this.

Yes, it requires an actual flight, unlike the other four. Worth mentioning anyway, because Blackberry Farm is the benchmark a lot of the industry measures itself against — tucked into the Smokies, with a food and wine program serious enough that people plan entire trips around specific tasting menus. Flexible enough for a couples trip or a family one.

The real takeaway

"Off the beaten path" and "roughing it" are not the same thing. All five of these prove you can disappear from everyone you know for a few days without giving up a single creature comfort.
